# The weekly mix

Three to five posts. Spread them across the week rather than stacking them on one day.

## Shape of a normal week

| Share | Kind | Source |
|---|---|---|
| About half | The realtor's own activity | A listing event from the last seven days: coming soon, just listed, open house, price improvement, under contract, sold |
| About a quarter | Local market | A number from Propaired's market data for an area they actually work, with the period it covers |
| About a quarter | Useful or human | Advice a buyer or seller genuinely asks about, a neighbourhood they know well, their own take on something |

Rules of thumb:

- **One listing, one post.** Five posts about the same house reads like an ad campaign, not a
  presence.
- **Never two of the same kind back to back.** Alternate activity and non-activity posts.
- **A sold post needs the sale to be closed** and the seller to be comfortable with it.
- **A week with no listing events is normal.** Fill it with market and useful posts rather than
  stretching one listing into four posts or recycling last week's.

## What each post needs

- A reason it exists this week. "Just listed" is a reason; "content" is not.
- One idea. A post that makes two points makes neither.
- One clear next step, at most — a question, an open-house time, a link. Not all three.
- The brokerage name.
- Only facts that came from a tool result.

## What not to post

- A price, a status or a figure you did not read from a tool result.
- Anything about who lives in an area or who a home would suit.
- Compensation or commission terms.
- A client's name, face or situation without the realtor confirming they agreed.
- Another agent's listing as though it were theirs.
- The same copy on every network. Cross-posting is fine; identical phrasing everywhere reads as
  automated.

## Timing

Weekday mornings and early evenings suit most of this audience. Keep an open house or a showing
announcement far enough ahead to be useful — the day before, not the hour before.

If the realtor has a view on timing, theirs wins; ask once and save it with the knowledge-base
routine so the question does not come up again.
